---
title: Trust Signals
description: On-page elements and external references that signal credibility, authority, and reliability to both human readers and AI evaluation systems.
---

**Trust signals** are indicators that a website or page is credible and authoritative. They include both on-page elements (author bylines, publication dates, references, privacy policy) and off-page signals (backlinks from authoritative domains, Wikipedia citations, verified social profiles).

## Why they matter for AI search

AI models increasingly evaluate content trustworthiness before citing it. Pages with strong trust signals are more likely to be selected as sources for AI-generated answers, while anonymous, undated, or unverified pages are deprioritized regardless of content quality.

## Key trust signals

- **Author identity** — Named authors with credentials or bio pages
- **Publication dates** — Clearly visible dates with "last updated" where relevant
- **Transparency pages** — About, Contact, Privacy Policy, Terms
- **External validation** — Wikipedia links, industry citations, peer references
- **Contactability** — Real physical address, email, or phone
- **HTTPS** — Secure connection (table stakes, but absence is a strong negative)
