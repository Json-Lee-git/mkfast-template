## About AEOCheck

AEOCheck is a product and website for website owners,
marketers, developers, and technical SEO teams who want to understand how
ready their sites are for AI-powered discovery systems.

AEOCheck publishes its guides and methodology under the organizational byline
**AI Search Readiness Editorial Team**. That team is part of AEOCheck's
publishing operation. When the same team is listed as author and reviewer, it
does not represent an independent review by a separate organization.

The project focuses on measurable readiness signals: crawl access, LLMs.txt
availability, sitemap discoverability, structured data, page structure, entity
clarity, and trust signals that answer engines can parse.

### What we offer

- **LLMs.txt Checker** - Validate your LLMs.txt file, AI crawler access,
  sitemap, and LLMs-full.txt availability.
- **LLMs.txt Generator** - Create a clean, AI-readable LLMs.txt file from
  your sitemap or manual inputs.
- **AEO Checker** - Run a technical answer engine optimization audit covering
  crawlability, structured data, content structure, entity clarity, and trust
  signals.
- **Query Fan-Out Tool** - Simulate how AI search systems may decompose a
  broad query into sub-queries, intent clusters, and content gaps.

### How our checks work

Our tools inspect public technical signals that a crawler or answer engine can
reasonably access. The AEO Checker currently evaluates the signals documented
on the [methodology page](/methodology). Other AEOCheck tools have narrower,
tool-specific scopes.

- HTTP status, canonical URL presence, robots.txt, sitemap, and documented AI
  crawler rules.
- LLMs.txt and LLMs-full.txt presence, format, and discoverability.
- JSON-LD schema types such as Organization, WebSite, Article, FAQPage, and
  SoftwareApplication.
- Page structure signals such as headings, lists, concise answer blocks, and
  internal links.
- Trust signals such as author information, dates, about/contact/privacy
  pages, and transparent publisher information.

These checks are technical readiness signals. They do not guarantee rankings,
citations, AI Overview inclusion, or traffic. Search engines and AI answer
systems use many signals that are not visible from a public page audit.

### Editorial standards

Guides and glossary pages are written to be practical and falsifiable. When we
make technical recommendations, we aim to distinguish between:

- **Directly checkable facts**, such as whether a page has JSON-LD or whether a
  crawler is blocked in robots.txt.
- **Best-practice guidance**, such as using clear entity names, dated content,
  and descriptive internal links.
- **Uncertain AI search behavior**, where no public platform gives complete
  ranking or citation rules.

We avoid promising "AI SEO magic." If a recommendation is based on a technical
signal rather than a guaranteed platform rule, we describe it as readiness
guidance rather than a certainty.

### Updates and corrections

AI search behavior changes quickly. We review core tool pages, glossary pages,
and guides when major crawler, schema, or search platform changes affect the
advice. Content pages show publication metadata, and important updates are
reflected in the page metadata where supported.

If you find a technical error, outdated recommendation, or unclear explanation,
please use the [contact page](/contact) or the support email listed there. We
accept correction requests as product quality feedback, but do not promise a
specific response or correction time.

### Our philosophy

- **Free core tools.** The main checkers and generators are available without
  requiring sign-up.
- **Actionable results.** Audit findings should point to a specific fix.
- **Transparent limitations.** We check signals that are visible and
  measurable, and we say when something cannot be guaranteed.
- **Human-readable and machine-readable.** Pages should be useful to people and
  clear enough for search systems to parse.
